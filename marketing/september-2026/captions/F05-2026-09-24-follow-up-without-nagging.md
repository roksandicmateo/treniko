## CAPTION — Instagram

> Nobody teaches this part, and it is most of the job between sessions.
>
> The difference between a check-in that gets answered and one that gets left on
> read is usually not the wording. It is whether the message refers to something
> the client actually told you, and whether it arrives on a rhythm they can
> recognise.
>
> "Just checking in" asks the client to do the work of remembering the context.
> One specific question, tied to something they said, asks for one line back.
>

## CAPTION — Facebook

> Three rules for following up with a client without feeling like a nag.
>
> 1. Tie it to something they said. "You mentioned Thursdays are hard" gets an
>    answer. "Just checking in" gets left on read.
> 2. Keep a fixed rhythm. Every second Monday is a system. Messaging whenever you
>    happen to remember reads as pestering, even when it is not.
> 3. Ask one question they can answer in one line. Three questions gets you
>    nothing at all.
>
> None of this needs software. It needs you to remember what they said, which is
> exactly the thing that gets lost when the notes live in six different threads.
>
> What is the check-in message that works for you?
