# TRENIKO — Instagram content strategy

Audience: independent and hybrid personal trainers running roughly 10–40 active
clients. They are not shopping for software. They are tired of admin. Content
has to name the admin problem before it names the product.

Positioning: **Run your coaching business. Not your spreadsheets.**
Support: *Clients. Sessions. Payments. Progress. One place.* · *Less admin. More coaching.*

## 3. Content pillars

Four permanent pillars. Every post belongs to exactly one.

### Pillar 1 — Trainer business pains
The pillar that earns shares, because it describes the reader's Tuesday.
- "Signs your coaching business has outgrown spreadsheets"
- "Where does your coaching business actually live?"
- "The five apps between you and one simple answer"
- "The admin you do after the last session ends"

Test: would a trainer send this to another trainer with "this is us"?

### Pillar 2 — Practical coaching business tips
Useful with or without TRENIKO. This is the pillar that earns saves and buys
the right to sell in the other three.
- Client onboarding: what to ask in the first session
- Following up without nagging
- Retention: the check-in cadence that keeps clients past month three
- Pricing packages so they are simple to explain and simple to track
- What to do when a client goes quiet

### Pillar 3 — Product-led
Real screenshots, real workflows, real vocabulary — Dashboard, Calendar,
Trainings, Clients, Packages, Exercises, Groups, Progress.
- Before/after organisation
- One workflow, start to finish
- Small features that remove a whole recurring annoyance

Rule: never mock up a screen that does not exist, and never fabricate numbers
inside a screenshot. Use realistic but obviously generic client names.

### Pillar 4 — Relatable trainer content
Professional enough for a SaaS brand. Wry, never sloppy, never meme-template.
- "POV: a client asks how many sessions they have left"
- "Checking three apps to answer one question"
- "That message at 21:40 asking to move tomorrow's session"

Tone ceiling: a smile of recognition, not a laugh. If it needs a meme font, it
is not on brand.

**Mix:** roughly 40% product, 25% pain, 25% practical tips, 10% relatable.
Never two consecutive product posts.

## 4. Reels formula

One reusable 15-second structure. Reels are the only realistic reach engine for
a new account; the grid converts, Reels acquire.

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | Trainer-specific hook | Name the audience or the situation in the first three words. On-screen text, readable without sound |
| 2–6 s | The pain, shown | Show the fragmentation — thumbing between apps, scrolling a spreadsheet. Show, don't caption |
| 6–12 s | The product | Screen recording of the real app doing the one thing the hook promised. One task, start to finish |
| 12–15 s | CTA | One line, one destination. "treniko.com" |

Worked example:
> **Hook:** "You have 20 clients and still track sessions in Notes?"
> **Pain:** swiping between a chat thread, a calendar and a spreadsheet
> **Product:** open the client, see sessions remaining, book the next one
> **CTA:** "There is a better way. treniko.com"

Production rules:
- Every frame legible with sound off. Assume silent autoplay.
- Screen recordings from the live app only. No fabricated functionality.
- Hook text on screen within 0.5 s. No logo intro, no countdown, no "wait for it".
- Vertical 1080 × 1920. Keep text out of the bottom 250 px, where the caption
  and UI sit.
- One idea per Reel. A second idea is a second Reel.

Ten hooks ready to shoot:
1. "You have 20 clients and still track sessions in Notes?"
2. "How many sessions does this client have left? Go. Ten seconds."
3. "Your coaching business lives in five apps. Here is all of it in one."
4. "The part of coaching nobody signed up for."
5. "Rescheduling one session should not take four apps."
6. "What your spreadsheet cannot tell you."
7. "Onboarding a new client, start to finish, in under a minute."
8. "You are not disorganised. Your tools are."
9. "The 21:40 message every trainer knows."
10. "Admin after the last session ends. Every night."

## 5. Growth principles

Growth comes from content trainers choose to spread, not from mechanics.

**Do**
- Publish consistently — three posts and two Reels a week beats a burst
- Write captions that stand alone; many people never see the image
- Answer every comment and question in the first hour
- Give away the useful thing in the post itself
- Reply in the trainer community as TRENIKO where it is genuinely helpful

**Never**
- Follow/unfollow cycling, mass DMs, bought followers, engagement pods or bots
- Invented statistics, user counts, revenue claims or testimonials
- Fake scarcity, countdowns, or "only 3 spots left"
- Engagement bait ("comment YES"), or hashtag stuffing
- Any language other than English in public copy

The account is small and honest. It should stay honest — a SaaS brand that
inflates its numbers at 1 follower has nothing left to trade on at 10,000.
