### C04 · Thu 24 Sep 2026 · 18:00 · Carousel ×4 · Practical tips

**HOOK** — How to follow up without feeling like a nag.

**ON-SCREEN COPY**
1. `How to follow up` / `without feeling like a nag.`
2. `Tie it to something they said.` / `"You mentioned Thursdays are hard —" beats "just checking in".`
3. `A fixed rhythm beats a random poke.` / `Every second Monday is a system. Whenever-you-remember is nagging.`
4. `Ask one question they can answer in one line.` / `Three questions gets you zero answers.`

**CAPTION**
> "Just checking in" is the most ignored message in coaching.
>
> It puts the whole job on the client: work out why I messaged, work out what I
> want, write me something. Most people will do that later. Later never arrives.
>
> A follow-up that references something specific they told you does the opposite
> — it proves you were listening, and it can be answered with four words.
>
> The rhythm matters as much as the wording. If your check-ins land on a
> schedule, they're part of the service. If they land whenever you happen to
> think of it, they feel like chasing — to them and to you.
>
> What's your cadence?

**CTA** — Save this. Steal the wording.
**HASHTAGS** — `#ptbusiness #coachingtools #clientmanagement #lessadmin #personaltrainer #fitpro`
