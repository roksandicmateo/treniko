| Free-resource roundups | Pages listing free templates for trainers | ⚠️ Slow. Needs an email per site, most go unanswered. Non-zero |
| Guest contribution | thePTDC | ⚠️ Pitch, not submit. Months. Highest quality if it lands |

### Not realistic, and worth saying so

**TRENIKO will not build meaningful link authority in the next six months, and
nothing free changes that.** The sites that link to software either get paid, get
a relationship, or link to something that already has an audience. TRENIKO has
none of the three yet.

What actually moves the needle at this stage is **being useful in a place where
trainers already are** — which is § 2, not § 3. The tracker is the only asset
with a real chance of being linked, because it is free, needs no email, and is
genuinely usable.

**Never:** purchased links · PBNs · link farms · automated submission · comment
spam · fake profiles · guest posts written only for the link.

## 5. Two posts you can publish as they are

Both are true, both are useful to the reader without TRENIKO, and TRENIKO is
context rather than subject. That is what makes them acceptable in the
communities that allow them at all.

### For r/SaaS or Indie Hackers — the prerender post

> **Title:** Prerendering one page of a Vite SPA without adding a framework
>
> Our marketing page was a client-rendered SPA, so the HTML served at `/` was an
> empty `<div id="root">`. Google runs JavaScript, but on a second pass with a
> rendering budget a brand-new domain does not command — and most other crawlers
> do not run it at all. The one page every external link pointed at was the one
> page with nothing in it.
>
> The fix was about forty lines and no new dependency: `react-dom/server` (already
> present via react-dom) rendering the landing page once at build time into a
> static file, using Vite's existing `--ssr` mode.
>
> The part that is not obvious is that you need **two** HTML files. If the
> prerendered homepage is also the SPA fallback, `/login` arrives with landing-page
> markup already in `#root`, React tries to hydrate a login form onto a landing
> page, and every private route starts with a hydration mismatch and a flash of
> the wrong page. So: `index.html` for `/` only, and an untouched `app.html` for
> every other route, with nginx routing the two apart.
>
> Two things I got wrong afterwards, both caught in production rather than review:
>
> 1. The SPA fallback still said `index, follow`, so every invented URL was an
>    indexable 200 serving an empty shell. Classic soft-404 fan-out.
> 2. The build step also has to strip the hardcoded canonical from the fallback,
>    or every unknown URL claims to be the homepage.
>
> Happy to share the build script if it is useful to anyone.

*(No link needed. If someone asks, answer.)*

### For r/SaaS or Indie Hackers — the analytics post

> **Title:** Counting page views first-party, no cookie, no third party — and what you give up
>
> We wanted to know which pages bring signups without adding Google Analytics or a
> Meta pixel. What we ended up with is one table, one endpoint, and a beacon that
> stores no identifier of any kind.
>
> What it does store: path, referrer **host** (not the full referrer — that can
> carry someone else's query string), and the four UTM fields. What it stores about
> the person: nothing. No cookie, no localStorage, no fingerprint, no IP.
>
> **What you permanently give up:** unique visitors. With no identifier there is
> nothing to deduplicate by, so every number is page loads. That is a real loss and
> the admin panel says so on the page rather than quietly presenting views as
> visitors.
>
> Two things that surprised me:
>
> - Downloads served straight off disk by nginx never touch the app, so the most
>   important step of our lead-magnet funnel was invisible until we added a click
>   beacon.
> - Organic search carries no UTM — nobody tags a Google result — so all of it
>   was landing in `(direct)` next to people typing the address in. Splitting
>   untagged traffic by referrer host was a one-query fix for a metric that had
>   been meaningless for weeks.
>
> No consent banner needed for any of it, which was the point.

## 6. Social → website funnel

### What exists

- **Instagram** `@treniko_fitness` — 10 posts published, 14 more scheduled to
  mid-September
- **Facebook Page** *Treniko* — 7 feed posts + 4 Reels scheduled
- Cycles 1 and 2 total ~54 written pieces, through 17 Oct

### The problem, stated plainly

**All 54 pieces were written before the website had anything to link to.** The
content is not the bottleneck; the connection is. Six "Story · Link · Website"
slots in cycle 2 have no destination chosen, and the bio link is the only
persistent path from profile to site.

### Fix 1 — the bio link. One tap, highest leverage on this list

Set it to the free tracker and **leave it there**:

```
https://treniko.com/free-personal-trainer-client-tracker?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=organic&utm_content=link-in-bio
```

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It asks for nothing.** No account, no email, no card. A profile visitor who
   is not ready to sign up still leaves with something — and a trainer who
   downloads a client tracker has self-identified more precisely than any
   targeting could.
2. **The page already sells honestly.** It explains what a spreadsheet cannot do
   and links onward. That work is done; the link just has to reach it.
3. **It is stable.** A bio link that changes weekly builds no recognition, and
   rotating it per post throws away the only persistent path you have.

**And it is now measurable**, which it would not have been last week: the
download click writes its own row, so tracker-page views versus downloads is a
real conversion rate rather than an assumption.

### Fix 2 — give the six empty Story slots a destination

No date changes, no copy changes, no touching the 14 scheduled posts.

| Slot | Send to | Why that page |
|---|---|---|
| ST04 (20 Sep) | `/guides/new-client-first-week` | Follows R02, "onboarding a new client" |
| ST08 (26 Sep) | `/free-personal-trainer-client-tracker` | Follows R04 on the week's schedule |
| ST12 (3 Oct) | `/guides/pricing-personal-training-packages` | C06 that week is about explaining package prices |
| ST16 | `/guides/cancellation-policy` | Pair with any cancellation piece |
| ST20 | `/guides/session-packages` | The account's most-repeated hook |
| Reel reshares | bio link | Do not compete with the bio |

Tag every one: `?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=organic&utm_content=story-<id>`

⚠️ Instagram rewrites outbound links through `l.instagram.com`, so the referrer
shows that. **The UTM tags are what actually attributes the visit** — an untagged
Story link is recorded as direct and the slot is wasted.

### Fix 3 — six pieces for the tracker, which has no social coverage at all

Written and final in `marketing/social/RESOURCE_DISTRIBUTION.md`: a carousel on
the four columns that stop package arguments, a single on the "how many sessions
has she got left" hook, a 15-second Reel of the countdown formula, the weekly
check carousel, an honest anti-spreadsheet-post single, and a research poll.

### What is deliberately not proposed

No new pain-point content — 54 pieces exist and none has finished publishing. No
ads, no boosts, no paid promotion. No urgency, scarcity or social proof: the
account has two followers, and every piece above works at that size because none

### The uncomfortable conclusion

**The first ten users will not come from SEO.**

Everything built this session and last — twelve pages, the tracker, the schema,
the headers, the analytics — is a compounding asset that pays out in months, and
it will keep paying out for years. It is the right thing to have built. It is
not the thing that produces user number one.

User one comes from **#2: a trainer you already know**, in a conversation, this
week. User two through ten come from **#1**, after weeks of being a useful member
of a room where trainers already talk to each other.

The honest sequence:

1. **This week** — message every trainer you know personally. Not a pitch: *"I
   built the thing I kept complaining about; would you tell me where it is
   wrong?"* Three replies is a successful week.
2. **This week** — Search Console, ten minutes. It does not produce a user; it
   starts the clock on the channel that eventually does.
3. **This week** — set the bio link. One tap, and it makes 54 scheduled pieces
   point at something.
4. **Next two weeks** — join three Croatian trainer groups. Read the rules. Post
   nothing.
5. **Within a month** — the Capterra hour.
6. **Then wait.** The content needs two to four months before Search Console has
   anything worth reading, and writing more pages before then is guessing twice
   instead of once.

**What would make this fail:** treating the SEO work as the acquisition plan. It
is the foundation. The first ten users are a conversation, and that part only you
can have.
